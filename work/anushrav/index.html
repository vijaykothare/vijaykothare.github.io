Ānushrav

One website that sells a marketing service and a software product, without confusing either.

Client
Ānushrav, Mumbai
My role
UX strategy, UI design, responsive web
Platform
Responsive website
Year
2026
Overview

Ānushrav is a Mumbai company that runs ads for small businesses and then gives them the Ānushrav Practice Suite, one app for leads, bookings, payments, campaigns and reports. I designed the website that has to explain both offers to busy owners of clinics, studios and service businesses, and turn their interest into a demo booking.

01Context

Small service businesses in India often hire an agency for ads, then manage the enquiries those ads produce on WhatsApp, in spreadsheets and through payment links sent by hand. Leads slip through, and nobody can say which rupee of ad spend produced which paying client.

Ānushrav closes that gap by doing both jobs. That is its strength, but it also creates the design problem: two offers on one site can easily read as two different companies.

02The problem

Most agencies stop at the click. Most software starts after it. The site had to show what happens in between.

  • Visitors are owners, not marketers. They decide in seconds whether a site is 'for them'.
  • Two offers (service and software) compete for the same attention.
  • Software is abstract until you see it working on your own kind of business.
  • Trust is the real barrier: handing over ads and client payments to a new partner is a big step.

03Goals

  • Make the combined offer clear in one sentence
  • Show the product in action without a sales call
  • Give every visitor a low-effort next step
  • Speak to each type of practice in its own words

04Who it's for

Practice ownersConsultants, clinics, studios, coaches and local services who already advertise but lose enquiries.
Their teamsFront-desk and admin staff who handle bookings, reminders and payments every day.

05Structure & flow

I organised the site around the path an enquiry takes: an ad brings it in, the Suite books and collects, and the owner sees the return. Each section answers one question in the order an owner would ask it.

  • Hero: the promise, plus a live product mock-up
  • What we do: 01 Service and 02 Software as two clear cards, followed by 'Better together'
  • Platform: 13 tools in one sidebar and six core screens
  • Who it's for, how we work, FAQ and a single demo booking form

06Key design decisions

01Lead with one promise'Marketing that brings clients. Software that keeps them.' joins both offers in a single line before either is explained.
02Show, don't describeThe hero mock-up follows one enquiry from an Instagram ad to a WhatsApp chat to a paid ₹999 UPI booking, so the product is understood at a glance.
03Indian context as trustUPI, cards and netbanking, WhatsApp follow-ups, festival offers and rupee amounts tell owners it was built for how they work.
04One primary action'Book a free demo' is the main action throughout, with WhatsApp as the low-effort alternative for people not ready to fill in a form.

07Mobile first

Most owners will first see the site on a phone, often straight from an ad. Every section was designed to stack cleanly, keep tap targets large and keep the demo button within reach.

08Visual system

A warm terracotta palette and an editorial serif set Ānushrav apart from the blue of typical SaaS sites and suit the personal service it sells. Italic accents ('keeps', 'finding', 'trust') add warmth. Dark bands mark the moments where trust matters most: who it's for and how the team works.

09Outcome

One mobile-first site carries both offers, makes the path from ad to booking visible, and gives every visitor an easy next step, whether that's a demo or a WhatsApp message.
