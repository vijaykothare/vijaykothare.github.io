FoodFeed

A food app that cuts the 30–40 minutes people spend deciding what to eat.

Client
Concept project
My role
UX research, UI design, usability testing
Platform
iOS & Android
Year
2021
Overview

FoodFeed is a food-ordering app that brings menus from many delivery platforms into one place, with a social layer: see what friends are eating, ask them for advice, and reorder quickly. The project followed a full design-thinking cycle, from interviews to a redesign based on feedback from 50 users.

Deliverables
Interviews & empathy mapsPersonasUser flows & wireframesUIUsability testing with 50 users

01The problem

Working professionals spend 30–40 minutes on average just deciding what to eat.

They switch between apps to compare menus, lose track of offers, and end up spending more time and money than planned.

02Research

  • Interview goal: understand how people with busy schedules plan meals, and where ordering through several apps frustrates them
  • Target users: 15–56 years, tech-savvy, often living away from home, ordering daily or occasionally
  • Pain points: decision time, switching apps, missing discounts, and wanting to know what friends order
  • Empathy maps captured what users say, think, do and feel

03Personas

Ankit Singh, 19Engineering student in Mumbai, away from home. Wants to save time and money and stay healthy while studying.
Srushti Diwakar, 29Junior manager in Pune who lives alone. Confused by long menus and short on time; wants healthy, tasty food without the hassle.

04Flows & wireframes

A user flow mapped the journey from sign-up through browsing, ordering, tracking and rating. Low-fidelity wireframes then tested the structure before any visual design.

05Key design decisions

01Context-aware homeA greeting with time and weather, and lunch picks suited to the moment, shortens the 'what should I eat' loop.
02Friends as a filter'Your friends are having now' shows real orders with ratings, a faster signal than reading reviews.
03Ask before you orderFriend Zone chat lets you ask a friend about a dish without leaving the app.
04Order againPrevious orders are one tap away, because most meals are repeats.

06Usability testing

After the first phase, the design was shared with 50 users and feedback was collected in a structured sheet.

What workedEasy to use · assorted picks loved · thoughtful approach · quick to order · chat feature
What didn'tGreen too loud · Add to Cart hard to read · search grabbed attention · layout could improve · wanted a camera in chat

07Iteration

  • Loud green header turned into a white card, and header colour toned down to a pastel shade
  • Add to Cart contrast increased for legibility
  • Order summary reordered to go 'start to end': store, then delivery address
  • A 'Remove all items' action and a distinct colour for offers and events
  • Tab bar adapted for full-screen phones

08Outcome

Testing directly shaped the final design: calmer colour, clearer actions and a more logical checkout, while keeping the social features users valued most.
