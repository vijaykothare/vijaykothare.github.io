Edumentr

A study-abroad app that takes students from 'where do I start?' to a submitted application.

Client
Edumentr
My role
UX research, UI design, interactive prototype
Platform
iOS & Android
Year
2023
Overview

Edumentr helps students explore universities and courses abroad, compare them, and apply from one app. I designed the full journey, from sign-in and discovery to course details, filters, profile, document upload and application tracking, and built the prototype you can tap through below.

01The problem

Applying abroad means juggling university websites, agents, spreadsheets and documents.

Students often don't know which courses fit their budget and profile, and lose track of where each application stands. Parents, who frequently pay, want to see costs and progress clearly.

02Who it's for

First-time applicantsNeed guidance on what to do next, not just listings.
Comparison shoppersWant to filter by fees, country, intake and language requirements.
ParentsWant transparency on cost and progress.

03Key journeys

  • Sign in with a mobile OTP, or Google, Facebook or Apple, with 'Skip for now' to browse first
  • Discover: top universities, trending courses and success stories on the home feed
  • Explore by subject, then course details with duration, fees, intake, eligibility and process in tabs
  • Filter by course, country, fee range, study level, mode, language test and intake
  • Apply: choose countries, complete a profile, upload documents and track every application

04Key design decisions

01Browse before sign-up'Skip for now' lets students explore before committing, which lowers the cost of trying the app.
02Resume, don't restartA persistent 'Your application is incomplete · Progress 20% · Resume' card brings students back to unfinished work.
03Tabs for dense detailCourse pages split Course, Eligibility, Application Process and Visa into tabs, so long content stays scannable.
04Friendly edge casesClear empty, 404 and success states ('Application submitted successfully') reassure at stressful moments.

05Visual system

A deep teal and warm orange palette feels trustworthy and optimistic. A subtle hexagon pattern adds texture without competing with content, and the orange is kept for the moments that matter: offers, progress and primary actions.

06Outcome

An end-to-end mobile flow from first launch to submitted application. The Figma prototype covers more than 30 screens; it runs live below.
