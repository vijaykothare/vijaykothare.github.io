UniTrans

Every line, one journey: Mumbai's trains, metro, monorail, bus and ferry in one app.

Client
UniTrans by V K Enterprise, Mumbai
My role
Founder · product design, UX/UI, app & website
Platform
Android, iOS & web
Year
2026
Overview

UniTrans is my own product: a transit app for Mumbai and Pune that puts local trains, metro, monorail, BEST and city buses, ferries and auto and taxi fares in one place. It plans journeys with changes, shows the platform and door side, gives fares before you go, and shows where your train actually is, using positions shared by riders on board. I designed and built the Flutter app for Android and iOS, the website at unitransapp.in, and the service behind it. It is launching soon.

01Context

There's no official live feed for Mumbai's locals. UniTrans builds one from the riders on them.

Millions of people in Mumbai ride the locals every day, then change to a metro, monorail, bus or auto. Each mode has its own app, timetable or none at all, and changing between them is guesswork.

There is no official live feed for Mumbai's local trains. A timetable says 8:12, but the train on the platform is often running late, and the commuter can't tell by how much.

02The problem

  • Journeys cross modes (train to metro to monorail) but apps plan one mode at a time
  • Countdowns follow the timetable, not the real train, so 'in 6 min' is rarely six minutes
  • Platform numbers and door side are what people check on the platform, yet they are buried or missing
  • Fares differ by class (2nd, 1st, AC), operator, and day or night for autos and taxis
  • Signal drops underground and in tunnels, so an online-only app fails exactly when it's needed

03Who it's for

Daily commutersSame train every morning; want the platform, the real delay and an alert before it leaves.
Interchange ridersTrain to metro or monorail at Dadar, Kurla, Thane or Andheri; need real change times and walking links.
Occasional travellersUnfamiliar lines and fares; need one place that explains the whole city.
Riders who feel unsafeNeed help in one tap, with location ready to share.

04Goals

  • Answer the platform questions at a glance: which train, which platform, which side, how late
  • Plan one journey across every mode, with honest change times
  • Make live train positions possible without an official feed, and keep riders private
  • Work fully offline, with live data as a bonus, not a requirement
  • Earn money without ever getting in the way of catching a train

05Key design decisions

01Home that answers 'where to?'A greeting, the city switch, one big 'Plan journey' card and a live status board ('Western ~3 min late') sit above mode tiles for Train, Metro, Monorail and Bus.
02Results built for scanningEach train is a row with the time, destination, Fast or Slow tag, fares, ride time and arrival, and the platform number in large type on the right, with a live countdown under it.
03Colour carries meaningFast trains get a green rail, slow trains amber, AC a blue tag; a highlighted row marks the train leaving now. Filters for Fast, Slow, AC and Ladies sit on top.
04Direct or with a changeA two-way switch moves between direct trains and journeys with changes, which use real change times and walking links between nearby stations.
05Platform and door sideThe platform number is shown in big type, with which side the doors open, for both the boarding and the getting-off station.
06SOS in one tap112, railway police, women's and child helplines, with location ready to share. Railway numbers come first near a station.

06Live trains, from the people on them

A rider taps 'I'm on this train'. Their phone shares its position only while the app is open, and only for that train. UniTrans combines several riders, so one bad GPS reading can't move a train, and everyone sees the real delay in countdowns, the line status card and their alerts.

  • Private by design: positions are kept for two minutes, and other riders never see who shared
  • Every report is checked against the line, accuracy and speed, and each device earns a trust score, so spam loses weight without anyone being silently banned
  • Riders who share are thanked: an impact counter ('Your trip helped 1,240 riders today'), 24 hours ad-free per verified trip, streaks and badges with anonymous nicknames
  • When signal drops in tunnels or underground, trains move from 'live' to 'recent' to 'estimated', so the map never pretends to know more than it does

07Ads that never get in the way

Commuter apps in India usually live on ads, so they had rules from day one.

Where ads goOne native ad styled like a row, after the 3rd train and never above the next one; one banner on Fares and Line status; a card on Home shared with station-area sponsors.
Where they never goNo interstitials or video between screens, and nothing on SOS, sign-in, the live map, or while 'I'm on this train' is on.

08The website

unitransapp.in introduces the app before launch: the promise 'Every line. One journey.', every line it covers as chips, six feature cards drawn as small illustrations (journeys with changes, where your train is, platform and door side, fares, alerts, SOS), and a three-step explanation of how live trains work.

09For brands and local businesses

A separate page speaks to advertisers in their own terms: 'Be there when commuters look up the next train.' UniTrans is opened on the platform, at the ticket window and on the walk to the station, several times a day. The page offers a small number of clearly labelled ad spots and station-area sponsorships across Mumbai and Pune, aimed at shops, coaching classes and clinics near stations, with two clear actions: plan a campaign or see the formats.

11Behind the scenes

A product like this has to be run, not just shipped, so I designed the service behind it too. Riders, website visitors and staff all reach one secure front door. Behind it, one service answers the app, the live train connection and the admin console, and a worker sends commute alerts and push notifications.

Accounts, reports, alerts and the audit log live in a database; live GPS positions are kept for only two minutes. Sign-in codes, push notifications, off-site backups and error monitoring come from outside services.

  • Admin console for staff, with two-factor sign-in: live operations map, report moderation, announcements with capped push alerts per line, versioned timetable uploads with roll-back, and a full audit log
  • Commute alerts arrive as push notifications even when the app is closed

12Status & what's next

UniTrans is in testing on Android and iOS, with the website live and a closed beta planned with 50–100 commuters on the Central and Western lines. Next on the roadmap: home and lock-screen widgets for 'My next train' and line status, and a UniTrans Plus subscription with no ads and unlimited alerts.
